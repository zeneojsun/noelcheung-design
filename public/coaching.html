01 Is this for you

Most designers aren’t blocked by talent.

They’re blocked by uncertainty, positioning, communication, and momentum — the things no tutorial quite teaches. If any of the left column sounds familiar, the right is what we’re working towards.

Right now

  • You’re applying everywhere and hearing very little back.
  • Your portfolio shows what you did, not why it mattered.
  • You freeze when asked to explain your thinking out loud.
  • You’ve been ready for senior for a while — no one has noticed.
  • You’re rebuilding after burnout, a long gap, or a layoff.
  • You want to move into fintech or crypto and don’t know how.

After our work

  • You apply to fewer roles, but you hear back from more of them.
  • Your case studies read as decisions, trade-offs, and outcomes.
  • You speak about your work clearly, without rehearsing every line.
  • You can name what senior actually requires — and where you stand.
  • You have a realistic next quarter, not a fantasy five-year plan.
  • You know which companies to target, and which to skip.

02 Approach

I’ve lived both sides of this conversation.

For most of a decade I led design at OKX, HSBC, and Melco — building systems, shipping product, hiring teams, sitting on the other side of the interview table. I know what hiring managers actually look for, because I was one.

And then I spent three years on the candidate side.

— 2022–25 · 200+ applications · primary research

Some of it was burnout from a design systems role no one was using. Some of it was the market. Most of it was the slow, grinding realisation that the things I’d been telling junior designers for years — about portfolios, about positioning, about how to tell the story of your work — were the same things I needed to tell myself.

That’s the work we do here. Not generic advice. Not another framework. A 1:1 conversation, structured around what you actually need next, from someone who has spent three years stress-testing this material on themselves.

Quiet confidence over noise. Practical over performative. The point isn’t to hand you answers — it’s to ask the question that makes the answer obvious.
